Financial Cyber Intrusion: The Long View

Tracking financial cybercrime across banking, securities infrastructure and cryptocurrency. The first baseline uses independently published historical series, not an invented composite score.

01 / FBI INTERNET CRIME · ANNUAL LOSSES

Reported U.S. losses, 2020–2025

$4.2B → $20.9B

Almost fivefold growth in the dollar value of internet-crime losses reported to the FBI from 2020 through 2025. The total includes fraud and other internet crimes, not exclusively sophisticated intrusions.

Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center annual reports · Complete-year 2026 figures not yet available.

02 / CRYPTO PLATFORMS · INCIDENT COUNT

Recorded hacks, 2020–2024

119 → 303 hacks

These are yearly platform hack counts in a historical Chainalysis series. Counts rise overall, but not every year. Personal-wallet thefts are a different category.

Source: Chainalysis historical hacking data. Later datasets have different coverage and cannot simply be appended.

03 / 2025–2026 BRIDGE · DIFFERENT METHOD

Current signals, not a seamless extension

2025: Chainalysis estimated more than $3.4 billion in crypto theft by early December 2025. This is a loss estimate, not a comparable hack count.

H1 2026: TRM Labs counted 207 crypto hacks totaling $972 million, versus 83 hacks in H1 2025 in the same TRM dataset. That is +149% in incident count for the comparable six-month periods. It is not a full-year total.

Sources: Chainalysis 2025 · TRM H1 2026

Important limit:

Increased dollar losses or hack counts do not establish that frontier AI is behind the activity. Crypto market value, growth in targets, reporting standards, conventional cybercrime and state-backed operations all matter. Banking and stock-exchange intrusion histories need comparable datasets before we can calculate a defensible combined index.

NEXT ENGINEERING STEP / PROPOSED INDEX

Financial Cyber Intrusion Index

Track three independent lanes: (1) banking and payments, (2) brokerage, exchanges and clearing infrastructure, (3) cryptocurrency platforms and custody. For each verified case, record incident date, first-disclosure date, losses, confirmed access method, third-party or insider involvement, AI evidence if any, and quality of attribution. Normalize by activity and reporting coverage before combining them.
