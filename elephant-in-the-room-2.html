🐘 The Elephant in the Room
Part 2: When the Network Goes Quiet

The NSA's classified internal network is reportedly down. It follows disruptive problems in aviation and cloud services. Does their timing mean something? It gives us a question—not an answer.

Current finding: No verified common cause.

We have multiple reported disruptions, but no demonstrated shared attacker, exploit, infrastructure dependency or coordinated mechanism. Coincidence remains a serious possibility. This investigation tracks evidence, not suspicion.

What we know

01 / NSA NET · REPORTED · CAUSE UNKNOWN

Classified internal communications outage

CNN reported that NSA Net, a classified system used for internal communications, experienced an outage. Reuters reported the CNN findings and sought comment from the NSA. The precise scope and cause remain unconfirmed publicly.

Not established: a cyberattack, an agency-wide intelligence blackout, or a connection to other incidents.

Source: Reuters · November 17

02 / FAA & CLOUD · SEPARATE INCIDENTS UNDER REVIEW

Aviation and Microsoft outages

Recent disruptions affected air traffic operations and Microsoft services. A federal official said investigators had not found evidence tying the incidents to a coordinated cyberattack. Microsoft said it was unaware of malicious activity behind its incident.

Not established: a shared cause between FAA, Microsoft and NSA Net.

Source: Reuters · November 17

03 / HISTORICAL BASELINE · DOCUMENTED

January 2000: NSA's earlier major network failure

NSA records describe a January 24–28, 2000 outage tied to a software anomaly. Its intelligence-processing systems were affected for about 72 hours while collection continued. The 2026 reported outage concerns a different described function; the failures should not be treated as technically identical.

Sources: NSA declassified assessment · Director Hayden's 2000 statement

How bad is it?

HIGH POTENTIAL OPERATIONAL IMPACT · 4/5

A classified communications outage could materially hinder operations, but this is a provisional editorial severity rating—not a measurement of disruption, evidence of hostile action, or a prediction. Severity will be revised if the reported scope proves narrower or broader.

What would change our assessment?

  • Escalate: confirmed prolonged loss of classified communications, documented mission impact, or independently corroborated malicious activity.
  • Connect incidents: matching forensic findings, proven shared technical dependencies, or an official investigation establishing a common cause.
  • De-escalate: documented restoration, an isolated technical root cause, or a finding that the outage was limited in scope.

The same-week timing is a clue to investigate, not evidence that one actor caused everything. No public attribution is made here.
